# 0024: Slice 4 plan (ownership layers)

Date: 2026-10-28

Status: **accepted 2026-10-29:** the user chose the recommended option for O1 to O7. Steps 1 to 6 are built; the exit check is note 0025. Builds on notes 0001, 0005, 0007, 0008 and 0009, and on BOR's `docs/kg-mapping.md`.

## What Slice 4 delivers

Roadmap: Owner Portfolio and Operational Network memberships with evidence paths; why buildings are grouped or not; conditions across a layer; what each absence means. Questions Q8, Q9, Q10, Q19, Q21 and Q26, all derived (Type II) except where they cite recorded facts. Slices 0 to 3 must still pass.

| Q | Contract | Answer shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q8 | Operational Network of a building: which buildings, which links connect them, whole component or Louvain community, the run | list with links |
| Q9 | Owner Portfolio footprint: lots, buildings and residential units, composition, boroughs, the signals behind each member, and how it differs from the Operational Network | counts and a comparison |
| Q10 | Where the layers disagree: a network holding several portfolios, a portfolio spanning several networks, and the evidence that explains each | list with evidence |
| Q19 | Why two buildings are grouped or left unlinked, in each layer: supporting records, stages, a connecting path, exclusions and the run | chain |
| Q21 | For a chosen layer, which buildings hold the most open violations per unit and how concentrated they are, and how the answer changes between layers | ranked list and a concentration figure |
| Q26 | For an address, which sources hold no record (HPD registration, DOS entity, either layer, any violation) and what each absence means in terms of the five reasons of note 0007 | list of absences with reasons |

Every answer names the run, the snapshot, the layer and the counting unit (requirements 1 and 2 of the competency questions), says the group is a lead and not legal ownership, and quotes a stage's precision only as a property of the method, with its interval and its adjudication status (requirement 5).

## What the source holds

The dependency in the roadmap is met: **BOR's release with edge-level evidence exists.** `beneficial-ownership-resolution/release/v0.2.1` (also zipped in `dist/` with a checksum) is run `2026-05-29-149e871f`, schema 0.2.1, built from snapshot `justfixwow-2026-05-29` (complete through 2026-05-29, the same snapshot id the graph uses), bor revision `1b122d14a9`, nlr `6a9b1a19ac`, Splink threshold 0.999, 31 tables, about 460 MB. The method's precision rests on a provisional gold (`human_llm_provisional`); the answer must say so.

- **Groups:** 16,196 (Owner Portfolios: 6,451 by identity, 61 deed-only, 11 deed-bridged; Operational Networks: 9,627 whole components and 46 Louvain communities). 46,547 group members (nodes), 114,986 (layer, group, lot) rows with a `rental` flag, 30,351 representative witness steps, 57,202 network edges before any split, and a node-to-component-to-network table.
- **Lots:** 43,556 lots are in both layers, 27,700 only in a network, 174 only in a portfolio. 476 networks hold more than one portfolio (the largest holds 14) and 14 portfolios span more than one network: the material for Q10.
- **Evidence:** 36,228 decision events (accepted, rejected, masked and excluded, with the rule, its version, score and threshold), 28,032 rows of the HPD contacts a decision rests on, 839 deed evidence items with 2,454 members, 1,713 link sets, and 73 masked aggregator addresses with the 4,117 nodes using them.
- **Absence:** `bbl_status` has 1,717,250 rows over the PLUTO lot universe. 1,355,542 of them are `no_registration` (a lot HPD does not register, `outside_source_coverage`), which BOR says a loader may skip and answer from its own HPD data. The 114,574 `in_group` rows are the memberships. The other 247,134 are absences: `no_linkage` 206,993; `no_owner_contact` and `unmatched_node` (`outside_method_input`) 19,180; policy exclusions 15,948 (masked or aggregator address, co-op or condo, institutional screen); below the eligibility rule (name and deed vetoes) 5,013.
- **The anchors are mostly unlinked.** Of the seven anchor lots only two are in a group. C (438 52nd Street) is in a 5-lot Owner Portfolio and a 5-lot Operational Network, so the earlier expectation that it would be excluded as a co-op does not hold in this release. D is in a 33-lot portfolio and a 35-lot network, which makes it the Q10 case (two lots differ between the layers). A, B, E and G are `no_qualifying_linkage` in both layers (A and B are the "candidate pair" of the detail document: unlinked), and F is `no_registration` (outside source coverage). The expected answers for Q8, Q9, Q10, Q19 and Q21 therefore also need groups found in the release itself, listed in the build order.
- **Names are in the release** (note 0009): `node_names` holds the landlord name of each node, as filed, and `groups.anchor_node_id` names the largest member by lot count, a display label that is never an owner.

## Decisions for the user

**O1. What to load from the release.**
- **(a) Everything needed to explain a grouping, recommended:** `groups`, `group_members`, `group_bbls`, `group_witness`, `network_edges`, `network_membership`, `node_observations` and its alternatives, `node_names`, `decision_events`, `decision_support`, `evidence_items` and `evidence_members`, `link_sets` and members, `masked_addresses` and their nodes, `method_metrics`, and the `bbl_status` rows that are absences other than `no_registration` (247,134). The manifest and the stage and reason vocabulary versions are loaded as the run. Only the nodes that sit in a group are loaded (46,547 of 118,493). Rough size: 50,000 nodes, 115,000 lot edges, 30,000 witness steps, 57,000 network edges, 36,000 decisions and 247,000 absence edges, so minutes, not hours.
- (b) Memberships only, as a smaller first step. Cannot answer Q19 or Q10's "what explains each difference".
- (c) Everything, including the identity-grain tables (`identity_clusters`, `identity_observations`, `node_identity_matches`, `identity_names`, `observation_contacts`, `deed_documents`, `deed_parties`, `lot_addresses`). The graph already holds the HPD contacts, deeds and lot addresses those tables repeat, and note 0007 argues identities should not become nodes; they add rows and name copies for no question.

**O2. Graph shape, recommended.**
- A `BorRun` node (key `run_id`) carrying the manifest facts, linked to the `Snapshot`. Every other BOR node carries the flat Type II provenance of note 0005 (`reliability: II`, `source_category: project_derived`, `review_status: unreviewed`, `source: bor`, `snapshot_id`, and the run).
- `BorGroup` (key `assertion_key`), with layer, composition or split, lot counts, and `anchor_node_id` as a property.
- `BorNode` (key the member `assertion_key`, the run-scoped node of note 0007 item 5), with `name` from `node_names` as display text, `IN_GROUP` to its group, and `COVERS` to each `Lot` it holds with the registration and an `ambiguous` flag. A node maps to the recorded role through `SELECTED_ROLE` to the `HpdRole` of the selected observation, and through the alternatives with a flag when the node is ambiguous.
- Membership of a lot is an assertion edge `(Lot)-[:MEMBER_OF]->(BorGroup)` with `link_id = <assertion_key>#<bbl>`, the `rental` flag and the provenance, using the `AssertionEdgeShape` core already defines for this.
- Evidence: `WITNESS_STEP` and `NETWORK_EDGE` relationships between nodes (stage, basis, weights, whether the edge crosses networks); `BorDecision` nodes with `SUPPORTED_BY` to the HPD roles; `BorEvidence` nodes (deed bundles) with their member lots and the ACRIS documents that the graph already holds.
- Absence: `(Lot)-[:NOT_IN_GROUP {layer, reason_code, top_level_reason}]->(BorRun)`.
- The alternative, flat property tables on `Lot` nodes, would make a path between two lots impossible to follow in the graph, which is the point of Q19. Expanding the edges onto identity nodes was rejected in note 0007.

**O3. Vocabularies.** An `absence-reason` SKOS scheme with the five top-level reasons of note 0007 (`no_qualifying_linkage`, `excluded_by_policy`, `below_eligibility_rule`, `outside_source_coverage`, `outside_method_input`) and BOR's detailed codes narrower than them, and a `link-stage` scheme for BOR's stages (`identity.splink_base`, `identity.registered_llc`, `identity.splink_feedback`, `identity.curated`, `network.address`, `network.identity`, `network.name`, `deed.held_since`, `deed.linked_successor` and the rest in the release), each with BOR's own code as a hidden label and the release's `stage_vocabulary_version` and `reason_vocabulary_version` recorded. **Recommended.** The alternative is to keep BOR's codes as plain strings, which breaks the tech-stack rule that coded values are vocabulary terms.

**O4. Verifying the release before loading, recommended.** Implement BOR's checks in the loader (`etl/bor.py`): the schema version; every file's checksum against `manifest.output_checksums`; `snapshot_id` form and the snapshot date against the graph's own load; the row counts and fingerprints of the six required source tables against the justfixwow database (the fingerprint SQL is copied from BOR's documentation, not imported); every `assertion_key` starts with the run id; no `synthetic`, `excerpt` or `dirty` flag. Refuse on a mismatch. The alternative, trusting the manifest, would let a release built from a different dump load against this graph without a warning.

**O5. Names and the group anchor, recommended.** `node_names.name` becomes a `name` property of the `BorNode` (display only, never a key, never a role), and the group anchor is a `BorGroup` property labelled "largest member by lot count", shown only for its run. The contact, identity, deed and lot-address name tables are not loaded (O1). Every answer that shows a name says where it comes from and that a group is a lead (note 0009).

**O6. Ambiguity and ties, recommended.** The 616 ambiguous nodes (642 lots) get a `COVERS` edge for each tied candidate, flagged `ambiguous`, and the 751 nodes that straddle clusters keep the selected cluster and a list of the alternatives with the votes; the answer says so rather than hiding the choice BOR made. The alternative, loading only the selected reading, hides the cases BOR itself flags.

**O7. Counting units, recommended.** Lots come from `group_bbls` (a node may hold several), buildings are the `Building` nodes on those lots in the graph (a lot can have several BINs, and an unregistered lot has none), and units are PLUTO residential units summed over the lots, with the number of lots missing a count reported (the contract's acceptable-incomplete case). The `rental = false` lots (co-op and condo, outside the published footprint) are counted apart. The answer never calls a lot count a building count.

## Risks

- **Reading a group as ownership.** The largest risk. Mitigation: the prompt rules, the judge, and tool output that carries "lead, not ownership", the run and the precision status in every result.
- **Run and snapshot drift.** The graph's snapshot and BOR's must match; O4 refuses otherwise. A new snapshot needs a new frozen release and a rebuild.
- **Hubs.** A few groups are large (up to 424 lots; 96 above 100). Tools return counts first and a bounded member list with the true totals, as Q13 does.
- **Few anchor cases.** Only C and D are grouped, so the evaluation relies on release-derived cases, which are less familiar but are checkable against the CSVs.
- **Provisional gold.** Precision figures are `human_llm_provisional`; the answers say so, and no figure is shown as the chance a link is right.
- **Absence edges.** 247,000 `NOT_IN_GROUP` edges are small, but `no_registration` rows (1.36M) are skipped on purpose; Q26 answers those from the graph's own HPD data ("no HPD registration", outside source coverage).

## Built (2026-10-29)

**Step 1, vocabularies, ontology, verification.** Four vocabularies: `absence-reason` (the five top-level reasons and BOR's 24 detailed codes under them, with BOR's definitions), `link-stage` (BOR's twelve stages and two sampled populations), and two small additions to O3 that follow from the same rule that coded values are vocabulary terms: `decision-outcome` (accepted, rejected, masked, excluded, dropped) and `gold-status` (none, synthetic, human_llm_provisional, blind_human). The notation of each is BOR's own code as the release writes it (`x-bor` hidden label), and `etl.vocab.bor_term` refuses a code the vocabulary lacks. `ontology/bor.ttl` and `bor.shapes.ttl` define `BorRun`, `BorGroup`, `BorNode`, `BorDecision`, `BorEvidence`, `BorLinkSet`, `BorMaskedAddress` and `BorMetric` (all Type II) and the edges below. `etl/bor.py` verifies a release before a load: schema version, no synthetic, excerpt or dirty flag, snapshot id and date against the graph, every file's checksum, every key beginning with the run id, the row counts and fingerprints of the six required source tables (two more warn), and the snapshot-date basis. Run on v0.2.1 against the justfixwow database, every check passed.

**Step 2, loader and load.** `etl/bor_build.py` (module `bor` of `etl.run_build`; `--verify-only` checks and loads nothing). Citywide result: 1 run, 16,196 groups, 46,547 nodes, 114,986 memberships, 114,986 node-to-lot edges, 114,759 selected contacts (642 ambiguous rows select none and list their tied candidates instead), 30,351 witness steps, 57,202 network edges, 36,228 decisions, 839 deed bundles, 1,713 link sets, 73 masked addresses, 20 metrics and 246,722 absences. Tests load a cut of the release (`tests/fixtures/bor_cut`, made by `scripts/cut_bor_release.py`, under a test run id and with placeholder names) in a rolled-back transaction.

**Choices made while building, none of which changes an accepted decision:**
- **Assertion edges.** MEMBER_OF, NOT_IN_GROUP, WITNESS_STEP and NETWORK_EDGE carry `link_id` and the Type II provenance (the links BOR asserts between lots and records). The others are structural (a BOR object to a BOR object or to the recorded row it cites). shacl2cypher does not allow `sh:node` on a relationship focus, so the properties of `wl:AssertionEdgeShape` are spelled out in each edge shape (a comment in `core.shapes.ttl` now says so).
- **Co-op and condo lots outside the footprint** (`excluded_from_footprint`, 412 rows) keep their MEMBER_OF edge with `rental` false and the reason `coop_condo_excluded`; they are not absences. `no_registration` rows (1,355,542) are not loaded, as O1 said, and the tools answer them from the graph's own HPD data.
- **Ambiguity.** An ambiguous (node, lot) row has no selected contact in the release. It is a COVERS edge flagged ambiguous plus a CANDIDATE_ROLE edge to every tied contact. A straddling node keeps `cluster_alternatives` (cluster, votes, selected).
- **Masked addresses.** The 73 aggregator addresses are nodes; a lot left out of a network whose landlord record uses one carries `masked_address_key` on its NOT_IN_GROUP edge, so the explanation can name the address and how many records share it. Only 358 of the 4,117 records that use a masked address sit in a group, so this key is how the rest are explained.
- **Names.** The landlord name is `node_name` (display only). The group anchor is `anchor_node_id` on the group and the tool names it as the member with the most lots.

**Steps 3 to 6, the tools.** `get_owner_portfolio` and `get_operational_network` (Q9, Q8; `agent/tools/layers.py`), `explain_why_grouped` (Q19), `compare_ownership_layers` (Q10), `rank_violations_in_group` (Q21; `agent/tools/layer_violations.py`) and `explain_what_is_missing` (Q26; `agent/tools/absences.py`), each with a prompt rule. Every result names the run, the snapshot and the layer, counts lots, buildings and PLUTO units apart (with the lots missing a unit count), quotes a stage's precision only with its interval and the gold status ("not evaluated" where the release has none), and says a group is a lead and an absence is not evidence. Expected answers are derived from the release files and the source database (`evals/expected/derive_slice4.py`); `tests/test_expected_slice4.py` checks the citywide graph against them (48 tests), and 26 evaluation examples cover the six questions.
