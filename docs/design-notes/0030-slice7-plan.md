## What Slice 7 delivers

Roadmap: the Deep Agents investigator, from an address to a dossier with an inference → source table, unresolved leads and a stop reason. Question Q20, the integration scenario, scored as a whole. Stage 1 is complete when Q20 passes and every earlier slice still passes.

The end-to-end requirement (competency questions, "End-to-end acceptance requirement") says: starting from an address, identify the property and its recorded entities; follow leads that meet the stated rule through public records, filing attachments and web sources, including free registries in other jurisdictions; produce a dossier in which every claim cites a source, retrieval time and source category and recorded assertions are kept apart from derived or reported leads; list unresolved leads with reasons; end with one of the stated stop reasons. Its seven checkable parts:

1. **Lead rule.** A lead is a new entity name or address in a recorded or retrieved source, not masked as an aggregator or mail drop, within the step and time budget. Everything else is logged and not followed.
2. **Claim-to-source.** Every claim has a source record or URL, a retrieval time and a source category. No claim states ownership on the strength of a derived layer or a group's person anchor.
3. **Labels.** Each claim is a recorded assertion (Type I), a derived lead (Type II) or a reported claim, with its review status. A "hypothesis" is a derived or reported lead, never a finding.
4. **Unresolved leads.** Each lead not followed or not resolved is listed with its reason: masked address, no further record, needs payment or inaccessible, budget reached, conflicting records.
5. **Stop reason.** Exactly one of: reached a registered-agent, mail-drop or masked address; no further recorded link; next source needs payment or is inaccessible; budget reached; conflicting records left unresolved.
6. **Other jurisdictions.** A recorded entity registered elsewhere is looked up in that state's free registry; a paid step is a lead with the reason "needs payment".
7. **Reproducibility.** The dossier records the run, the snapshot dates and everything retrieved, so the investigation can be replayed from the cache.

## What exists

Every component the investigator composes is built and tested: address to lot (Q1), HPD owner, agent and officers (Q2, Q4), deed against owner (Q3), DOS (Q5), deeds, mortgages and history (Q12 to Q14), conditions (Q15, Q17, Q27, Q28), the two ownership layers and their explanations (Q8 to Q10, Q19, Q21, Q26), address checks, shared evidence, affiliations, restructuring deeds, entity identity and agent footprints (Q6, Q7, Q11, Q18, Q22, Q23), reports and registries (Q16, Q25). That is 27 agent tools, a retrieval cache with replay, and expected answers for each. What does not exist is anything that decides **what to look at next**, keeps a ledger of what was found and why, and ends.

Facts that shape the design:

- **The anchors now give real trails.** A: owner `5413 PLAZA LLC` (DOS 5857246, process name the entity itself, no registered agent), a shared HPD business address with B that no method flags, the shared head officer and agent, the joint ConnectOne mortgage, a shared DOS process address (five entities), in no BOR group. The detail document's expected stop for A ("recorded trail ends at the entity's own process address; the shared HPD business address needs the mail-drop check") predates Slices 4 and 5 and has to be re-derived in step 5 from what the tools now return.
- **Leads exist in volume.** A single lot yields owner names, officer names, agent names and addresses; a hub address or common name could branch without end. The lead rule and the budget are what keep the investigation finite.
- **Web text is untrusted**, and the live search returns many data-aggregator pages; the investigator must keep reported claims apart from recorded facts.
- **`deepagents` is not installed.** The current documentation (read 2026-10-29, partly) shows `create_deep_agent(model, tools, system_prompt, subagents, middleware, interrupt_on, ...)`, dict subagents with `name`, `description`, `system_prompt`, `tools` and an optional `model`, a built-in planning tool (opt-in `TodoListMiddleware`), a virtual filesystem, a `task` tool for subagents, and a required checkpointer for interrupts. The pages I could read do not cover `response_format`, recursion or step limits, or resuming an interrupt; step 1 reads those and the API reference before any code.

## Decisions for the user

**I1. Who decides, and who enforces.**
- **(a) An orchestrating Deep Agent that proposes, and a code-enforced ledger that disposes, recommended.** The agent plans and chooses which tool to call next. Everything the contract checks is done by code behind tools the agent must use: `add_lead` applies the lead rule (masked or mail-drop address, duplicates, budget) and returns followed, queued or set aside with the reason; `record_claim` requires the ids of earlier tool results as its sources, takes the source category and time from those results (the agent cannot upgrade a claim), and refuses an ownership statement resting on a derived layer or a reported source; `finish` accepts only one of the five stop reasons and only when the ledger supports it (for example "budget reached" only if the budget is spent). The agent cannot write a claim the contract forbids, so the dossier rules do not depend on its good behavior.
- (b) A fixed LangGraph pipeline with a model only inside nodes. Reproducible and cheap, but not an investigator: it cannot choose a new lead or decide a trail is exhausted.
- (c) A free Deep Agent with a good prompt and a validator that checks the dossier at the end. The cheapest to write, but a violation is found only after the work is done, and the prompt then carries the whole contract.

**I2. The dossier and its vocabularies.** Recommended: a typed `Dossier` (Pydantic) holding the run, the snapshot and as-of dates, the subject, the claims, the leads and their dispositions, the stop reason, the budget used and the retrieval log; rendered to Markdown with the **inference → source table** the roadmap names (each claim, its label, review status, source ids, retrieval time and source category). It is a session artifact written to `data/dossiers/<id>/` (JSON and Markdown, gitignored), never to the graph. New small vocabularies, as everywhere coded values are stored: `claim-label` (recorded_assertion, derived_lead, reported_claim), `stop-reason` (the five), `lead-disposition` (followed, queued, set_aside, resolved, unresolved) and `lead-kind` (entity_name, address). The unresolved-lead reasons reuse `lead-reason` (note 0028) with additions: `masked_address`, `mail_drop_address`, `no_further_record`, `conflicting_records`, `budget_reached`, `person_not_followed`. The alternative of a free-text narrative with a checker is rejected: the claim-to-source and label rules need structure to be checkable.

**I3. What counts as a lead and where it goes next.** Recommended: **entity names and addresses only.** A person's name is logged in its recorded role and is not followed (mission: no personal information, names never assert a role); a company that the person's role connects (a registered owner, a managing agent) is a lead because it is an entity. Per kind: an entity name goes to DOS, the records-by-name search, shared evidence and, when DOS shows another jurisdiction, that state's registry (Q25) and the report search (Q16); an address goes to the aggregator check (Q6) first and is followed only if no method flags it, then to the other parties at that address. Defaults, all parameters stated in the dossier: at most 40 tool calls and 12 minutes in total, at most 6 leads followed beyond the subject, web at most 5 searches and 10 fetches per lead. Stop reasons are decided in code in this order: a lead set aside as a registered-agent, mail-drop or masked address with nothing else open; no further recorded link (queue empty); the next source needs payment or is inaccessible; budget reached; conflicting records left unresolved. The alternative of following person names would widen the investigation into individuals and is not recommended.

**I4. Reproducibility.** Recommended: every tool call is made through a logging wrapper that gives the result an id, a time, a source category and a content hash; the dossier carries the full log. A **replay** re-runs each logged call against the graph and the retrieval cache in replay mode and checks that the result hashes match; it does not re-run the model. The model's choices are therefore not reproduced, but every claim's evidence is, and a replay that finds a difference (a changed graph, an expired cache) says so. This is the strongest guarantee that does not require a deterministic model. The alternative of seeding the model and hoping for identical runs cannot be promised with this provider.

**I5. Structure and safety.** Recommended: one orchestrator with the ledger tools and the read-only graph tools, and two isolated subagents: `graph-researcher` (graph tools only) and `web-researcher` (`search_reports` and `lookup_registry` only, so web text is read in a context that has no ledger and no write path, and returns a short structured report). The filesystem tools are limited to a scratch area, `execute` is off, and nothing writes to the graph. No human-in-the-loop interrupts by default (the work is read-only); an interrupt is used only to offer more budget when it is spent, and needs the checkpointer. The alternative of a single flat agent puts untrusted web text and the ledger in one context.

**I6. How Q20 is scored.** Recommended: the whole, as the spec says. A deterministic validator over the dossier checks the seven parts (every claim has sources with time and category; labels match the sources; no ownership statement on a derived or reported source; each unresolved lead has a reason; exactly one stop reason and the ledger supports it; a foreign recorded entity has a registry step; the replay matches), plus a model judge for the narrative against the tool results. The cases: A (the main trail), C (no group exclusion, a co-op-looking owner), D (a lot with three buildings, no DOS record), F (no registration: the trail ends at once), a lot whose owner is a **Delaware** entity (to exercise the registry lead), and a lot whose trail reaches a mail-drop address. Web calls replay recorded responses so the score does not depend on the day; one live run per release is kept as a smoke check. The expected stop and the expected claims for each case are derived from the tools' own results on the snapshot, in the style of the earlier expected files, and the detail document's Q20 example is updated.

## Risks

- **Runaway or empty investigations.** The lead rule, the call and time budgets, and the stop-reason checks bound the work; the dossier states the budget used.
- **A claim without a source, or a stronger label than its source.** Made impossible by `record_claim` taking labels and categories from the cited results.
- **Reading a derived layer as ownership.** The refusal of ownership statements on derived or reported sources, and the existing prompt rules in each tool.
- **Prompt injection.** Web text is read only in the web subagent, which returns a fixed structure, and no page text becomes a query, a lead or a claim unless it passes the lead rule and cites its result id.
- **Cost.** An investigation makes dozens of tool calls and several model calls; the budget is stated, and evaluation uses replayed web responses.
- **Model variance.** Two runs of the same case can follow different leads. The score checks the contract (sources, labels, reasons, one stop reason, replay), not that the same trail is followed, and the expected files name the claims every good run must contain.
- **Framework churn.** Deep Agents and LangGraph change quickly; step 1 reads the current documentation and the code is kept to the documented API.
- **People.** Only entity names and addresses are followed; people appear in the role the record gives, with its source.

## Step 1 findings and build (2026-10-29)

- **Framework.** `deepagents` 0.7.32 installed with `uv add deepagents`. `create_deep_agent` takes `model`, `tools`, `system_prompt`, `middleware`, `subagents` (dicts with `name`, `description`, `system_prompt`, `tools`), `backend`, `interrupt_on`, `response_format`, `checkpointer`, `store` and `name` (the installed signature and the LangChain skills `deep-agents-core` and `deep-agents-orchestration`). The recursion limit is the LangGraph `recursion_limit` config (the run uses 150). Subagents are stateless and return one report, which is why the orchestrator's instruction to each is complete and asks for result ids.
- **Vocabularies:** `claim-label`, `stop-reason`, `lead-disposition`, `lead-kind`, and six terms added to `lead-reason` (`masked_address`, `mail_drop_address`, `no_further_record`, `conflicting_records`, `person_not_followed`, `already_followed`).
- **`agent/investigator/ledger.py`** (pure, thread-safe because the framework runs tool calls in parallel): the log (ids, times, labels, categories, hashes), claims (sources required, label = weakest source, verbatim quote for a reported claim, no ownership or control on a derived or reported source), leads (must appear in a retrieved result; a person, a masked or mail-drop address, a duplicate and anything past the budget are set aside with the reason), the stop reason computed by `compute_stop`, and `finish` refused while a lead is open, until a report search was made (budget excepted), and while a recorded entity registered outside New York has no registry step.
- **`agent/investigator/tools.py`:** the 25 graph tools and 2 web tools wrapped so each call is logged and returns its `result_id`; five ledger tools. **`agent.py`:** one orchestrator (ledger tools only) and two isolated subagents, `graph-researcher` and `web-researcher`. **`dossier.py`:** build, render with the inference to source table, save under `data/dossiers/`, and replay. **`validate.py`:** the seven parts and the Q20 components. **`evals/q20.py`:** six cases scored as a whole. 36 tests, none using a model.
- **Changes made after the first runs:** result ids and lead ids raced under parallel tool calls (locked); the call budget is reserved before a call runs; a vocabulary error in a tool argument is a refusal, not a crash; the reader's classification of a web quote is cached with the retrieval (and serialized across threads) so a replay gets the same reading; list orders in `get_affiliations` made deterministic so its hash is stable.
